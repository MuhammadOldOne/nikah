## 1. Audience size and structure: Muslim population, regions, age, marriage and divorce statistics

### Takeaway
No official count of Russian Muslims exists, because the census does not ask about religion. Credible estimates of "ethnic" or nominal Muslims who are Russian citizens are about 14–20 million (Pew: 16.4M, 11.7% in 2010). Muftiates claim 20–25M+ when Central Asian and Azerbaijani migrants are counted. The registered migrant pool shrank in 2024–2025. In the core North Caucasus republics, official marriages have fallen sharply while divorces have risen many times over since the mid-2010s. Chechnya, Ingushetia and Dagestan now have more divorces than marriages. That points to a large pool of divorced people looking for remarriage, though benefit-driven paper divorces distort the statistics.

### Inferences
- **Rough sizing (my estimate, not sourced):** take about 15–18M nominal Muslims among citizens (Pew's 2010 baseline plus faster growth among Caucasus peoples). Assume roughly 30–35% are aged 18–40 (the Caucasus population is younger than Russia's average; this is an assumption, see Gaps). That gives about **5–6M Muslims aged 18–40**. If about 35–45% of them are currently unmarried (never married, divorced or widowed), the unmarried pool is about **1.8–2.7M**. A religiously motivated segment open to online "halal" matchmaking might be 20–40% of that, so about **0.4–1.0M addressable users**, before adding migrants. Most Central Asian migrants are working-age men, so they would skew the gender balance of any open platform. Every percentage here is an assumption to be replaced with census age and marital-status tables.
- **Divorced and remarriage segment:** Chechnya, Ingushetia and Dagestan register more divorces than marriages, and Dagestan's divorces roughly tripled from 2014 to 2024. Remarriage of divorcees, especially divorced women with children, is a structurally large segment. Official numbers overstate real separations (benefit-driven paper divorces). They also miss religious-only marriages and talaq divorces, which never reach the civil registry office (ZAGS), so the true "remarriage-seeking" pool cannot be read from Rosstat alone.
- **Shrinking migrant pool:** the 2024–2026 crackdown reduces the migrant-Muslim segment and makes it riskier, both politically and legally. Products aimed at migrants should not be the core of the business plan.
- **Diaspora concentration:** Tatar and Bashkir communities, which are large but more secular and urbanised, are spread across the Volga, Urals, Siberia and Moscow. A Moscow-area diaspora estimated in the millions (unofficial) supports the thesis that people in small or dispersed communities need an online channel.

## 2. Pain points and current spouse-search behaviour of Russian Muslims

### Takeaway
Spouse search is still dominated by family networks, relatives, acquaintances and professional matchmakers (свахи, svakhi). Religious bodies have run paid matchmaking clubs since at least 2018, for example the Bashkortostan muftiate's youth "Ғайлә" club. VK and social networks are used, with some scepticism about trust. The main pain points are:
- strong social pressure to marry early, which leaves women "past age" with fewer options;
- a large and growing pool of divorcees, especially in the North Caucasus;
- a tightly regulated, family-mediated courtship process;
- polygyny, which is in demand among some men but legally and politically toxic: the DUM RF polygyny fatwa was withdrawn within days after the Prosecutor General's Office intervened in December 2024.

### Inferences
- **Trust is the core product problem.** Existing channels (family, svakhi, muftiate clubs) succeed because they vouch for people. Telegram and VK groups lack verification. The "Ғайлә" example shows users accept **paying for filtering** when it signals seriousness. A nikah site should therefore sell verification and family/wali involvement, not "swiping".
- **Priority segments supported by evidence:**
  - divorced men and women in the North Caucasus, because divorces exceed marriages;
  - women who are "past age" under early-marriage norms;
  - Muslims in diaspora regions (Volga, Urals, Moscow) where community networks are thin. These rely on VK, relatives and imams.
  - Converts are a plausible segment, but no sources were found (see Gaps).
- **Polygyny is a demand signal but a regulatory and PR landmine.** The Prosecutor General's reaction in December 2024 shows that openly facilitating "second wives" can draw prosecutorial attention, even though bigamy is not a crime. Russian law simply does not register a second marriage. Any "polygyny-friendly" feature would need careful legal framing, or should be avoided.
- **Regional differences matter:** the North Caucasus is family-mediated and conservative, while Tatarstan, Bashkortostan and the Volga are more secular and urban. Muftiate-endorsed models such as Bashkortostan's appear more accepted in the Volga.

### Gaps
- No quantitative survey was found on how Russian Muslims meet spouses (share via family, svakhi, mosque, Telegram, Instagram, dating apps), and no mufti statement explicitly on a "marriage crisis" or on online nikah or online matchmaking (fatwas on nikah via video call, etc.). Searches of the Dagestan muftiate statements returned nothing relevant.
- No sources were found on converts' difficulties finding spouses, or on Telegram nikah/matchmaking channels in Dagestan and Chechnya (sizes, pricing of svakhi). ## 4. Regulation a Russian nikah website must comply with

### Takeaway
A nikah site processes **religious-belief data by design**, which is a special category under 152-FZ. It therefore needs explicit, separate consent (mandatory as a separate document since 1 September 2025), storage of data on Russian servers (tightened from 1 July 2025), notification of RKN as a personal-data operator, and protection against breaches (turnover-based fines since 30 May 2025). Since 1 December 2023 Russian sites must authenticate users by approved methods, such as a Russian phone number, and may not rely on foreign email for registration. Any built-in messaging can bring the service into RKN's register of "organisers of information dissemination" (the ОРИ register), as happened to Mamba, Badoo and Tinder; that means storing user communications and giving security services access. No dating-specific Gosuslugi-verification law was found as of October 2026. Only a ZAGS-registered marriage has legal force, and a second registered marriage is barred. Advertising on Instagram and other blocked resources has been banned since 1 September 2025.

### Cited Findings

**Personal data (152-FZ)**
- **Separate consent document:** **Federal Law No. 156-FZ of 24 June 2025** amended Article 9(1) of 152-FZ. **From 1 September 2025** consent to personal-data processing must be **issued separately from other documents** (it cannot be embedded in an offer, terms of service or contract). Mandatory contents are listed in Article 9(4). — [Buhexpert8](https://buhexpert8.ru/1s-buhgalteriya/kadry-i-zarabotnaya-plata/kadrovye-dokumenty/s-1-sentyabrya-2025-oformlyajte-soglasie-na-obrabotku-persdannyh-otdelnym-dokumentom.html); [Buh.ru](https://buh.ru/articles/novyy-poryadok-oformleniya-soglasiy-na-obrabotku-personalnykh-dannykh-s-1-sentyabrya-2025-goda.html); [Garant](https://www.garant.ru/article/1862510/); [Robokassa explainer](https://robokassa.com/blog/articles/izmeneniya-v-152-fz-s-1-sentyabrya/); [Harant blog](https://harant.ru/blog/internet-pravo/chto-izmenilos-s-1-sentyabrya-2025-goda-v-oblasti-personalnyh-dannyh-dlya-onlajn-biznesa/)

### Inferences
- **Religious data is unavoidable.** Registering on a Muslim marriage site, plus fields such as madhhab, prayer practice or hijab, reveals "religious beliefs", a 152-FZ special category (see the UNVERIFIED Article 10 note in Gaps). Fields on polygyny attitudes and marital or sexual history may touch "intimate life", another special category. Design implications:
  - a **separate, explicit, written-form consent document** for special categories (separate from the general consent since 1 September 2025);
  - data minimisation;
  - a Russia-hosted stack with no Google Analytics, foreign CRM or foreign cloud for primary collection since 1 July 2025;
  - RKN operator notification;
  - breach-response procedures, given turnover fines.
- **ORI exposure:** if users can message each other in-platform, the site will likely meet the ORI definition. RKN has historically pulled dating services (Mamba, Badoo, Tinder) into the register on FSB request, and ORI status means data retention and FSB access. For a Muslim audience that is wary of security services after extremism prosecutions, this is a **trust and positioning issue**. Founders should plan for compliance rather than assume they can avoid it. An alternative is to route contact through a supervised wali or moderator flow, though it will still likely qualify.
- **Authentication:** Russian-phone-number authentication (406-FZ) is mandatory. Optional Gosuslugi/ESIA login could be offered as a trust feature, with real ID and marital status checks being the main pain point, but no law requires it for dating as of October 2026. The anti-fraud draft for ad platforms shows the direction of travel, and dating may be next.
- **Product framing:** the site should present nikah as a religious ceremony that **complements, not replaces, ZAGS registration**. It should encourage or require a declaration of civil marital status, and avoid marketing "second wives". The December 2024 precedent shows the Prosecutor General's Office reacts to institutional promotion of polygamy. The same caution applies to minors: the age gate should be 18+.

### Gaps (items below are UNVERIFIED in this session; based on background knowledge; must be checked on pravo.gov.ru / consultant.ru)
- **152-FZ Article 10:** special categories include racial or national origin, political views, **religious or philosophical beliefs**, health and **intimate life**. Processing is allowed, among other grounds, with the subject's **written consent** (Article 10(2)(1)). Under Article 9(4), electronic written consent requires an electronic signature, which is why services often use a simple e-signature or an SMS-code flow. Under Article 22 operators must **notify RKN** before processing, and RKN maintains the operator register; fines for failing to notify were raised in 2025. Whether religion inferred from "registered on a Muslim site" itself counts as special-category data is contested in practice and needs a lawyer's opinion.
- **ORI duties** (Article 10.1 of 149-FZ, Yarovaya amendments 2016):
  - store metadata for 1 year;
  - store message content for up to 6 months;
  - provide data and decryption keys to the FSB;
  - notify RKN.
  The fines and blocking regime need verification. Whether a site *without* user-to-user messaging falls under ORI was not verified. The current ORI register membership of Russian dating services (Mamba, Twinby, etc.) as of 2026 was also not verified.
- **2025 anti-fraud law (No. 41-FZ, April 2025)** and any ban on ORI registration using foreign phone numbers, with its effective date, was not verified.
- **Family Code Article 1:** only ZAGS-registered marriage is recognised. **Article 13:** marriage age 18, reducible to 16 by local authorities, and some regional laws allow lower in special circumstances. No criminal offence of bigamy exists in the current Criminal Code. All three are UNVERIFIED this session.
- **Extremism and banned organisations:** the content-moderation risk list was not researched this session (searches exhausted). It needs checking against the Justice Ministry's list. - **Payments and app stores** (not researched this session): Google Play billing for Russian users, App Store availability and Apple payments, RuStore (whose mandatory pre-installation on devices was reported for 2025), and the Mir/SBP payment rails. Needs a separate check.
- **Advertising law:** ERID labelling of online ads (since September 2022) and any restrictions on advertising religious services or "dating" were not verified. Whether the 3% ad levy applies to a small site's own ad sales was not verified.
